# User visual references — desktop art and interface direction

Reference: [Issue #12 — Megawatt valley inpiration](https://github.com/Mr-Meow-ZA/Megawatt-Valley/issues/12).

Reviewed 2026-10-07: both attached images at original resolution and six sampled frames across each of the two approximately ten-second clips. This was a visual review of stills, not a full motion or audio review.

## What the references establish

The target is a detailed, characterful, stylised 3D renewable-energy management game. Buildings, equipment, people and landscape should remain readable as a miniature valley at wide zoom and reward close inspection.

The images show an intentionally arranged operating campus: office entrances face paths, workshops open onto service yards, solar racks have service aisles, and substations occupy distinct fenced industrial compounds. Roads connect destinations and have consistent junctions, verges and frontage. Landscaping follows entrances, boundaries and terrain rather than uniformly scattering props.

Distinct building silhouettes and functional detail do much of the storytelling. The office has glazing, interior furniture and a planted frontage. The workshop has an open bay, tools, storage and maintenance vehicles. Substations have transformers, insulators and cables. Solar arrays stand on visible angled support frames.

Staff are recognizable people with readable work poses, clothing and tools. Vehicles and workers give the campus observable activity. Incidents have visible effects and staff responses. The clips communicate growth from a small solar operation into a substantial company through visible changes in the world.

Terrain supplies depth: river edges, rock faces, rolling meadows, foreground planting and distant hills. Warm sunlight, soft contact shadows and a consistent material palette tie the scene together.

The interface uses compact resource summaries, clear objectives, a horizontal visual building catalogue and contextual information. Icons and asset thumbnails carry more of the navigation than text-heavy menus.

## Implementation priorities

1. **Site composition and connections.** Establish road widths, junction rules, gate alignment, building entrances and service-yard layouts together. Every starter building should have understandable access. Validate adjoining road and fence geometry at close and wide zoom.
2. **Hero building and infrastructure detail.** Improve office, workshop, substation and solar rack models as one coherent family. Add purposeful functional props, material separation, windows, support structures and contact shadows.
3. **Visible work.** Improve crew silhouettes, walk/work poses, tools, repair and cleaning feedback, and vehicle routes. An observer should be able to identify where work is happening without opening a menu.
4. **Landscape depth.** Shape river banks, elevation transitions, rocky edges and grouped vegetation around the site composition. Preserve buildable land and placement readability.
5. **Interface composition.** Develop a horizontal asset catalogue with strong thumbnails, concise cost/trade-off information and contextual actions. Keep resources, current goal, placement feedback and time controls readable without competing with the world.

Evaluate each change with consistent screenshots at overview, normal play and close inspection zoom, including active placement and a maintenance incident. Check smaller laptop layouts as well as desktop resolution.

## Scope and interpretation

These references guide art direction and interaction hierarchy. Their wind farms, large city views and advanced campus imagery illustrate later progression; Here Comes the Sun remains the first delivery target.

Preserve the working road connectivity, service benefits, drag placement, right-click cancellation, staff jobs, scenario progression and save compatibility while improving presentation. Decorative placement should support visual coherence and retain honest gameplay descriptions.

Do not reproduce oversized persistent branding or arbitrary resource values from the reference compositions. The playable interface must prioritize the player's current task.

The current 0.4.0 desktop build supplies the 3D and interaction foundation, but its environment and assets are substantially simpler and sparser than these references. This document records a quality target and next work; it does not claim that target has been implemented.

Reference attachments are design inputs. They have not been incorporated as shipped game assets.
