# Megawatt Valley — Visual Direction v2.0

## Status

**APPROVED PRIMARY VISUAL DIRECTION — 29 September 2026**

The previous full-3D Unity visual target is archived.

The active production target is the pixel-isometric concept approved by Rapha on 29 September 2026.

## North-star statement

Megawatt Valley should look like a **polished contemporary indie tycoon game built around high-resolution pixel-isometric / illustrated pixel art**.

The target feeling is:

- warm;
- scenic;
- charming;
- technically recognisable;
- lively;
- optimistic;
- readable;
- premium rather than prototype-like.

The world should have pixel/isometric character while the management UI remains crisp, modern and non-pixelated.

## Primary benchmark

The approved concept shows:

- a lush mountain/river valley;
- modular solar farms;
- substation and grid equipment;
- offices and maintenance buildings;
- roads, fences and utility vehicles;
- small animated-looking staff;
- a modern dark-blue management UI;
- top-level cash / power / weather / time;
- objective panel;
- build menu;
- minimap;
- strong visual readability;
- rich but controlled environmental detail.

Future work should be judged against the **quality, composition and feel** of that benchmark, not against the archived 3D Unity direction.

## Projection and camera

- fixed or near-fixed isometric camera;
- 2:1 isometric projection;
- smooth pan;
- smooth zoom;
- no required rotation;
- clear depth sorting;
- readable placement grid where appropriate.

A single excellent viewing direction is preferred over multiplying asset work to support rotation.

## World readability

At normal zoom the player must immediately recognise:

- solar arrays;
- electrical infrastructure;
- roads;
- buildings;
- staff;
- vehicles;
- faults;
- buildable areas.

At close zoom, reward inspection with:

- staff activity;
- props;
- equipment details;
- signs;
- vegetation;
- small jokes;
- wildlife;
- maintenance effects.

## Environment

The environment should feel authored from reusable parts rather than painted uniquely for each screen.

Build reusable families for:

- grass / ground;
- dirt / service areas;
- roads;
- fences;
- cliffs / rock;
- river / water;
- trees and shrubs;
- small props.

Organic-looking scenery should emerge from modular composition.

## Renewable infrastructure

Renewable assets are the visual hero.

They should be more technically detailed than generic scenery while remaining stylised.

Priority families:

- fixed-tilt solar;
- inverters;
- transformers;
- substations;
- grid lines / pylons;
- small operations office;
- maintenance yard;
- service vehicles;
- cleaning equipment.

Later wind and BESS must use the same visual language.

## Characters

Characters should be small, distinct and economical to animate.

Target loops:

- idle;
- walk;
- repair/work;
- clean;
- reaction.

Role readability matters more than facial detail.

Use clothing, silhouette, tools and colour accents to distinguish jobs.

## Construction

Buildings / infrastructure should visibly progress through a small number of construction states rather than appearing instantly.

Example:

1. survey / markers;
2. groundwork / foundations;
3. structure;
4. equipment installed;
5. commissioned.

Four or five strong stages are enough.

## Weather and effects

Use inexpensive movement/effects to make the world feel alive:

- clouds;
- moving cloud shadows;
- rain;
- dust;
- wind;
- lightning where appropriate;
- panel glints;
- repair sparks;
- warning indicators;
- subtle vegetation motion;
- moving vehicles.

Effects should communicate gameplay state, not just decorate.

## UI

The management UI should be significantly sharper than the world sprites.

Visual language:

- dark/navy surfaces;
- clear white text;
- restrained green/yellow/red status use;
- rounded friendly panels;
- strong spacing;
- large readable values;
- compact icons;
- clear hover/selected states.

Primary surfaces:

- cash / income;
- power;
- weather;
- date/time/speed;
- objectives;
- build menu;
- selected object;
- staff / maintenance;
- event decisions;
- capability unlocks;
- stars / scenario progression.

Avoid making the UI look like industrial SCADA software.

## Asset consistency rules

All world assets must follow a controlled art bible covering:

- projection;
- pixel density;
- scale;
- lighting direction;
- shadow direction;
- saturation range;
- outline/edge treatment;
- ground-contact point;
- animation frame rhythm.

Do not mix obviously incompatible free asset styles.

Free assets may be recoloured, simplified or recomposed to achieve consistency.

## Quality target

The release should be capable of producing screenshots that feel like a real commercial indie management game.

It does **not** need:

- fully modelled 3D characters;
- free camera rotation;
- cinematic skeletal animation;
- photorealism;
- unique hand-painted terrain for every location.

It **does** need:

- cohesive art;
- rich renewable detail;
- clear gameplay information;
- lively activity;
- polished UI;
- intentional composition.

## Priority rule

When choosing between more detail and better readability:

> **readability wins.**

When choosing between unique one-off art and reusable coherent systems:

> **coherent reusable systems win.**
